Step by step

How a bulk order runs

From the first message to the boxes arriving. Nothing here is fixed in stone — a rush job compresses it, a complicated one stretches it.

  1. You send the brief

    Quantity, sizes, cloth and your logo — a WhatsApp message is enough to start. Add anything you already have: a design, a sample garment, a photograph of last year's uniform.

  2. Venkatesh quotes the job

    Priced on the real variables: how many, which cloth, how many colours in the artwork, print or embroidery. One number, explained.

  3. Cloth cut and stitched

    Uniforms are stitched to the measurements you gave, not pulled off a shelf and hoped for. Caps and hats the same.

  4. Your mark goes on

    Screen printed or embroidered, in the placement you approved. The first piece is checked against your artwork before the rest follow.

  5. Counted, packed, delivered

    The order is counted against your size list and packed. What arrives is what you asked for, in the quantity you asked for.

Two finishes

Printed or embroidered?

Both are done here. Which one suits your job depends on the cloth, the artwork and what the uniform has to survive.

Screen printing

Ink pressed through a mesh screen, one colour at a time, straight onto the cloth.

  • Best for large artwork and bold colour
  • Handles fine detail and photographs of designs
  • Sits flat — no bulk, no weight
  • The economical choice at high quantity

Embroidery

The mark stitched into the cloth in thread, raised off the surface.

  • Best for logos on chests, caps and collars
  • Reads as the more formal, premium finish
  • Outlasts the garment — it cannot crack or peel
  • Suits thicker cloth and workwear

✎ Not sure which one your job needs? Send a photo of the garment and the logo on WhatsApp — Venkatesh will tell you which finish he would use and why.
